Field of the Invention

Quantum Frequency Modulation (QFM):

The present disclosure relates generally to methods and systems for processing sequential symbolic data, and more particularly to a computer-implemented technique for deriving multi-token contextual relationships through the simulation of wave interference patterns within a fixed geometric lattice, without reliance upon recurrent loops or quadratic pairwise attention matrices.
Background

Conventional sequence models either process tokens recurrently (incurring sequential dependency and vanishing-gradient issues) or employ self-attention mechanisms that compute pairwise similarities across an entire sequence, producing computational complexity that scales quadratically with sequence length. There exists a need for an alternative feed-forward architecture capable of extracting long-range contextual signals while maintaining linear or near-constant scaling with respect to sequence length during the core propagation stage.
Summary of the Preferred Embodiment

In a preferred embodiment, a computing system receives an ordered sequence of discrete tokens. Each token is projected into a continuous harmonic waveform characterized by amplitude, angular frequency, wave-vector direction, and an initial phase offset derived from its ordinal position. The collection of waveforms is superimposed into a single collective wavefront field. This field is then propagated through a static, multi-dimensional array of passive observation nodes (the “holographic crystal lattice”). At each node the time-averaged energy density of the superposed field is measured. Nodes whose measured energy exceeds a predetermined activation threshold are classified as sites of constructive interference and are interpreted as encoding contextual affinity between the contributing tokens. Nodes falling below threshold are treated as sites of destructive interference and contribute negligibly to the output representation. The resulting sparse map of activated nodes is subsequently decoded by frequency-domain analysis and phase demodulation to recover an ordered sequence of output tokens.

The entire forward path is free of iterative recurrence and free of explicit pairwise matrix multiplications over the sequence length. Propagation through the lattice occurs as a single parallel wave pass whose dominant cost is linear in the number of tokens and constant with respect to lattice resolution once the lattice geometry has been fixed.
Detailed Description of System Components
1. Token-to-Wave Projection Module

A token embedding look-up table maps each vocabulary index to a deterministic angular frequency ωₙ. An amplitude Aₙ is assigned according to a significance metric (for example, a learned scalar or a static importance weight). The ordinal position pₙ of the token within the current sequence window determines an initial phase:

φₙ = (pₙ · π) / D_max

where D_max is the maximum supported sequence length of the embodiment. The resulting waveform for token n is expressed as:

Ψₙ(t, x⃗) = Aₙ · cos(k⃗ₙ · x⃗ − ωₙ t + φₙ)

In the preferred embodiment the wave vector k⃗ₙ is constrained to lie principally along a designated forward axis of the lattice so that all energy travels in a feed-forward direction.
2. Global Superposition Stage

All individual waveforms are summed at every spatial coordinate to form the collective field:

Φ_total(t, x⃗) = Σₙ Ψₙ(t, x⃗)

Because the summation is performed once per spatial sample and does not require pairwise interaction terms among tokens, the arithmetic complexity of this stage scales linearly with sequence length N.
3. Static Holographic Lattice

The lattice comprises a fixed three-dimensional (or higher-dimensional) grid of passive observation points L⃗_xyz whose coordinates are predetermined at design time and never updated during inference. Each observation point implements a simple energy accumulator that computes the time-averaged squared magnitude of the local field over a short observation window T:

R(L⃗_xyz) = (1/T) ∫₀ᵀ |Φ_total(t, L⃗_xyz)|² dt

A non-linear threshold function then produces a binary or soft activation:

ContextState(L⃗_xyz) = σ(R(L⃗_xyz) − θ_threshold)

where σ may be a hard step, a sigmoid, or any monotonic non-linearity.
4. Inverse Decoding Pipeline

Activated lattice nodes are re-projected into a continuous output wave field Ξ⃗(t). A localized Fourier analysis recovers the set of active frequencies and their associated amplitudes. Phase extraction followed by inversion of the positional mapping recovers the original ordinal indices. The highest-amplitude frequency for each recovered index is mapped back onto the vocabulary to emit the corresponding token. The process is entirely feed-forward.
Hardware Considerations

The preferred embodiment may be realized on conventional von-Neumann processors, on specialized digital signal processors capable of efficient trigonometric evaluation, or on analog computing substrates that physically realize wave propagation. Because the lattice geometry is static, the same set of observation nodes can be hard-wired or stored in read-only memory, further reducing run-time memory traffic.
Advantages Claimed by the Embodiment

    Elimination of quadratic pairwise attention matrices.
    Strictly feed-forward data flow with no recurrent state.
    Parallel evaluation of all tokens within a single wave-propagation pass.
    Fixed computational cost of the lattice stage independent of sequence length once the lattice resolution is chosen.

Limitations and Scope

The embodiment described herein is presented as a conceptual research framework. Empirical validation of training dynamics, gradient flow through the continuous wave operators, and large-scale language modeling performance remains outside the scope of the present disclosure. Practitioners are cautioned that the mathematical idealizations may require substantial discretization, approximation, and regularization before practical neural-network training becomes feasible.
