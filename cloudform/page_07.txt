6. Evaluation Considerations and Expected Performance


A rigorous evaluation of Cloudform should measure at least four quantities under realistic LEO contact models:
(1) time-to-first-usable-approximation (TTFU), (2) final reconstruction quality versus plane count, (3) goodput
under varying packet-loss and contact-window durations, and (4) end-to-end CPU/GPU mapping overhead
compared with conventional formats. Synthetic workloads should include dense weight tensors at 1-/4-/8-bit
quantization, sparse knowledge graphs of 10^4–10^6 nodes, and hybrid sensor archives.


Because the packing is deterministic, identical source artifacts produce bit-identical .cfb files, enabling
straightforward differential encoding and content-addressed caching across the constellation. Preliminary
analytic estimates for a representative 1-bit quantized 1 GB weight set indicate that the first 10–15 % of planes
often suffice for downstream classification or retrieval tasks whose accuracy saturates well before full precision
is restored, yielding effective bandwidth multipliers of 5–10× for many practical inference workloads.
