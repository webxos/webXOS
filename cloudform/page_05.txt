4. Progressive Reconstruction Semantics


Let B be a buffer of N elements quantized to Q bits. Under progressive emission the sender transmits planes
P_0, P_1, …, P_{Q-1} where P_k contains bit k of every element (MSB-first). After receiving the first k+1 planes
a receiver can form the approximation


B■_k[i] = Σ_{j=0..k} P_j[i] · 2^{Q-1-j}


which is a monotonically improving estimate of the original quantized values. For knowledge-graph or sparse
structures an analogous significance ordering is defined over edge weights or adjacency bits. Because each
plane is independently length-prefixed and hash-chained, the reconstruction process is robust to reordering at
the transport layer provided that the application re-assembles planes by index.


The progressive property has two immediate operational consequences. First, a ground station that obtains
only a fraction of a contact window still obtains a usable (if coarse) model or knowledge fragment that can drive
immediate inference or anomaly detection. Second, an LLM that is itself bandwidth-constrained when
exchanging large context or expert modules with a peer can begin useful computation on the first planes while
the remainder of the transfer continues.
