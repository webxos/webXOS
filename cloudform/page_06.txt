5. Integrity, Verification, and Loss Resilience


End-to-end integrity is provided by a final content hash (SHA-256 or stronger) covering all bytes after the magic
header, together with the per-plane running hashes. Optional erasure-coding parity blocks may be appended
when the ERASURE_HINTS flag is set; their size and placement are recorded in the header so that a receiver
can recover from a bounded number of lost packets without retransmission. When the SIGNED flag is set, a
constellation-root or ground-station signature over the content hash is included, enabling provenance
verification in multi-party orbital networks.


Under progressive mode the receiver can verify each plane as it arrives and abort or request selective
retransmission upon hash failure. This early-abort capability is particularly valuable on optical inter-satellite or
space-to-ground links where residual bit-error rates, although low, can still corrupt large transfers if left
unchecked until the end.
