1. INTRODUCTION


The emergence of orbital compute platforms—radiation-hardened TPU clusters
powered by multi-kilowatt solar arrays and cooled by passive radiative
surfaces—creates a new class of “megalith” data products: large, hierarchical,
self-contained knowledge and model packages that must be moved efficiently
between Low-Earth-Orbit (LEO) constellations and terrestrial ground stations
or cloud regions. Simultaneously, multi-agent and distributed LLM systems
increasingly require the transfer of large context windows, weight deltas, or
specialized expert modules under tight inter-model bandwidth budgets.


Conventional container formats (tar, zip, HDF5, standard GLB, Safetensors,
GGUF, etc.) were not designed for the joint constraints of:

  (i)   intermittent high-latency optical or RF contact windows,
  (ii)  extreme quantization down to 1-bit,
  (iii) the requirement that a partial download remain semantically useful, and
  (iv)  direct consumability by both radiation-hardened processors and modern
        GPU runtimes.

Cloudform Binary addresses these gaps by extending the proven length-prefixed
chunk model of GLB with progressive bit-plane ordering, GPU/CPU alignment
contracts, constellation routing metadata, and an intentionally LLM-parsable
header.


This document contributes:

  (1) a complete formal description of the .cfb layout and progressive
      semantics;
  (2) an analysis of the bandwidth and latency advantages under realistic
      LEO contact models;
  (3) a discussion of integrity and reconstruction under packet loss;
  (4) concrete implementation requirements, reference workflows, and a
      minimal working checklist; and
  (5) design recommendations for integrating Cloudform into both satellite
      flight software and terrestrial LLM orchestration frameworks.

2. DESIGN GOALS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

• Binary-first, zero-copy friendly, and SIMD/GPU-aligned
  Structures padded to 16/32/64/128-byte boundaries. Tensors and adjacency
  lists stored in GPU-preferred order (row-major or blocked).

• Artifact-centric
  Every file is a complete, hash-verifiable unit that can be staged, routed,
  or executed without external schema lookups.

• 1-bit pipeline ready
  Supports extreme quantization, bit-packed tensors, sparse decision
  structures, and progressive refinement so a partial stream remains useful.

• Cloud-bottleneck aware
  Minimizes random I/O, favors sequential streaming, embeds prefetch hints,
  and keeps metadata compact enough for edge LLMs and radiation-hardened
  processors.

• LLM-native
  Human-readable JSON-like header (still binary-packed) that models can emit
  or parse directly; supports “vibe” generation of entire megalith packages.

• Space-to-Earth optimized
  Optional constellation routing headers, erasure-coding hints, and
  deterministic reconstruction under packet loss.

• Generalized yet proprietary-friendly
  Open core structure with reserved vendor extensions for signed
  SpaceX/Starlink-grade integrity and inference acceleration.

3. RELATED WORK AND DESIGN CONTEXT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Binary container formats for machine-learning artifacts have proliferated in
recent years. Safetensors emphasizes zero-copy safety and simple tensor maps;
GGML/GGUF target quantized inference on commodity hardware; ONNX and
TorchScript focus on computational graphs. None of these formats natively
support ordered progressive refinement or constellation-aware routing.

Progressive image and video codecs (JPEG progressive, progressive mesh
transmission, scalable video coding) demonstrate the value of layered
delivery, yet they operate on continuous media rather than discrete knowledge
or weight structures.

In the space domain, CCSDS packet telemetry and the File Delivery Protocol
(CFDP) provide reliable transfer over deep-space links but treat payloads as
opaque byte streams. Cloudform sits at a higher semantic layer: it assumes an
underlying reliable or erasure-coded transport and concentrates on making the
payload itself progressively useful and GPU-ready.

The closest conceptual relatives are progressive mesh streaming in computer
graphics and bit-plane coding in successive-approximation A/D conversion.
Cloudform adapts these ideas to the specific needs of quantized neural and
knowledge artifacts under orbital constraints.
