1. Introduction


The emergence of orbital compute platforms—radiation-hardened TPU clusters powered by multi-kilowatt solar
arrays and cooled by passive radiative surfaces—creates a new class of “megalith” data products: large,
hierarchical, self-contained knowledge and model packages that must be moved efficiently between
Low-Earth-Orbit (LEO) constellations and terrestrial ground stations or cloud regions. Simultaneously,
multi-agent and distributed LLM systems increasingly require the transfer of large context windows, weight
deltas, or specialized expert modules under tight inter-model bandwidth budgets.


Conventional container formats (tar, zip, HDF5, standard GLB, Safetensors, etc.) were not designed for the
joint constraints of (i) intermittent high-latency optical or RF contact windows, (ii) extreme quantization down to
1-bit, (iii) the requirement that a partial download remain semantically useful, and (iv) direct consumability by
both radiation-hardened processors and modern GPU runtimes. Cloudform Binary addresses these gaps by
extending the proven length-prefixed chunk model of GLB with progressive bit-plane ordering, GPU/CPU
alignment contracts, constellation routing metadata, and an intentionally LLM-parsable header.


This paper contributes: (1) a complete formal description of the .cfb layout and progressive semantics; (2) an
analysis of the bandwidth and latency advantages under realistic LEO contact models; (3) a discussion of
integrity and reconstruction under packet loss; and (4) a set of design recommendations for integrating
Cloudform into both satellite flight software and terrestrial LLM orchestration frameworks.
