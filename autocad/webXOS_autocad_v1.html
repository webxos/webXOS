The 3D Assembly tab edits the mechanical scene; the PCB Design tab edits the board. The board and its components are also rendered live in the 3D viewport, so both views always stay in sync.

Modes

Object mode allows free transforms. Edit mode snaps moves to a 5 mm grid and rotations to 15°.

PCB routing

Pick the Route tool, click a pad to start, click empty copper to add waypoints — the live waypoint count is shown in the TRACE field of the status strip (up to 60). Finish by clicking a second pad, via or even an existing trace of another net; traces that land on existing copper merge nets automatically. Double-click or press Enter to leave an unterminated stub — DRC will flag it. Two-finger pinch zooms, right-drag pans.

DRC & nets

DRC checks clearance (configurable), off-board geometry, overlaps, thin traces, floating vias, split nets, single-pad nets, orphan copper and unterminated ends — click any result to flash its location. Unconnected pads are aggregated into a single summary row. The Nets panel lists every net including via-only nets; hover a row to highlight its copper, click to flash its pads.

Data

Save/Restore uses browser storage snapshots. Export/Import writes and reads a full project JSON. PNG captures the viewport, HTML writes a full project report.

PCB Wizard — guided board setup

Step 1 — Board setup

Define the physical board. Dimensions in millimetres; clearance is the minimum copper-to-copper distance the autorouter and DRC will enforce.

Two copper layers (top / bottom) with automatic via insertion when a net must change layers. Routing continues even if you switch browser tabs.

Step 2 — Components

Choose the bill of materials. Power nets (VCC / GND / VIN), the crystal, bus and IO nets are derived automatically from the pinout. The MCU reset pin is reserved and never borrowed as a generic IO.

1 component

Step 3 — Autoplace

The placer packs footprints onto the board: the MCU takes the centre, power and connectors hug the edges, passives shelf-pack around them.

Step 4 — Autoroute

A grid A* router with turn penalties and via costs routes every net on two layers. Placed vias stay passable for their own net, so later branches of a net can pass through them. Nets that cannot be closed stay as ratsnest and will be flagged by DRC.

Step 5 — Summary

Review the generated board, then apply it to the editor. The result stays fully editable — route, move and DRC as you like.
